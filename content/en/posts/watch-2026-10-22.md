First bulletin: it catches up on the major stories since 12 October. Future bulletins will only cover news from the last 24 hours and real developments in stories already being followed.

## The essentials

1. Two critical flaws in PHP have been massively attacked since 21 October: public websites were taken offline in France and across Europe.
2. Attacks increasingly target the physical world (supermarket refrigeration) and trusted tools (booby-trapped updates).
3. Ransomware: unpatched VPNs and firewalls remain the number one way in.

### PHP2Shell: two critical PHP flaws force websites offline across Europe

**Vulnerability · Critical**

**What happened**
Two flaws rated 9.2/10 (CVE-2026-109618 and CVE-2026-109619) affect PHP, the language that powers a large share of the web, including WordPress and Drupal. They allow an attacker to take control of a server remotely, without an account. Exploitation attempts were observed before the fixes were published on 21 October. In France, the Ministry of the Interior took prefecture websites offline as a precaution, at the request of ANSSI (the French national cybersecurity agency). The Netherlands and Finland took similar measures.

**Why it matters**
The flaw is in the language engine itself, not in an application: a well-coded website can still be vulnerable. According to Cloudflare, attack attempts went from a few thousand to several million within hours. The flaws were found in August by researchers who were using AI to help.

**Key takeaway**
Fixed versions: PHP 8.2.35, 8.3.36, 8.4.27 and 8.5.12. Older versions will not receive a fix, so migration is required. When a site cannot be updated right away, European authorities advise taking it offline.

*Sources: Recorded Future · Actu17 · ICTjournal*

### Supermarket refrigeration sabotaged: several hundred stores hit in France

**Industrial attack · Important**

**What happened**
In the night of 11 to 12 October, hackers took remote control of Danfoss cold-chain controllers and triggered continuous defrosting. Intermarché, Netto, Coopérative U, Leclerc and Carrefour had to throw away fresh and frozen goods, and fires were reported. No data was stolen and no ransom was demanded.

**Why it matters**
This is an attack on industrial equipment (known as OT, operational technology), not on office computers. Flaws in these controllers had been published as early as August, but the models concerned are end-of-life with no security patches according to Danfoss; the exact entry point is still debated. The controllers were reachable from the internet with a generic "Admin" account, and more than half of those exposed worldwide are in France.

**Key takeaway**
ANSSI considers it urgent to disconnect these controllers from the internet. The Paris prosecutor's investigation has been entrusted to the Office anticybercriminalité (the French police cybercrime unit). Perpetrators unknown, no claim of responsibility so far.

*Sources: Futura · ASTON École IT*

### 17 October: a wave of claimed leaks reportedly affects more than 9 million people in France

**Data breach · Important**

**What happened**
About twenty French organisations were named on the same day on hacker forums: Nomad Education (4.8 million records), I-CAD, the register of identified animals (2.2 million owners, unverified), the Centrale Canine, the French Shooting Federation, O'Tacos, Alain Afflelou and Malakoff Humanis. The same day, pro-Russian groups claimed denial-of-service attacks against ANSSI and the websites of major cities. The CAF (family benefits agency) website was unreachable for several hours, with no confirmed cause.

**Why it matters**
These are hacker claims, judged credible by the specialist site FrenchBreaches but not yet confirmed by the organisations. Contact details, ID documents and health data are then used for highly convincing scams.

**Key takeaway**
Be wary of emails and text messages that quote your personal information. Pro-Russian denial-of-service claims are often exaggerated: according to ENISA, fewer than a quarter are confirmed by independent measurements.

*Sources: Journal du Coin*

### A bad week for trusted tools: OVHcloud, AOMEI, Rank Math and Railway

**Supply chain · Important**

**What happened**
On 19 October, an attacker used an OVHcloud developer's GitHub account to replace the files of the official command-line tool (versions 0.11.0 to 0.15.0) with a program that steals secrets, for about 1 hour 30 minutes. The same week, booby-trapped updates of AOMEI Backupper and AnyViewer, then an infected version of the Rank Math PRO WordPress plugin, were distributed. On 21 October, the hosting provider Railway reported possible access to some customers' environment variables (API keys, database passwords).

**Why it matters**
These attacks go through tools that people install with full trust: an official update can contain malware. The main target is developers' secrets, which then open access to servers and the cloud.

**Key takeaway**
At OVHcloud, a GitHub protection that prevents modifying an already published release was not enabled; this has been fixed. If you installed these tools during the affected period, change the secrets present on the machine. Railway is asking its customers to change all their secrets without delay.

*Sources: SecurityWeek · IT-Connect · Cyber Security News*

### Ransomware: VPNs and firewalls remain the number one entry point

**Ransomware · Important**

**What happened**
The Qilin and INC groups are exploiting a Palo Alto Networks flaw (CVE-2026-0257) to bypass authentication on VPN connections (GlobalProtect), then go after virtualisation servers and backups. On 20 October, CISA and the FBI published an alert about the SafePay group, which has hit at least 600 critical-infrastructure organisations since 2024. On 13 October, Ivanti reported a flaw in its Connect Secure VPN that attackers were already exploiting.

**Why it matters**
The Palo Alto flaw has been fixed since May 2026: the victims are those who did not apply the patch. Internet-facing equipment (VPNs, firewalls) gives direct access to the internal network.

**Key takeaway**
Three defensive reflexes: patch internet-facing systems first, following CISA's KEV list; enable two-factor authentication everywhere; protect backups, because SafePay deletes backup copies before encrypting.

*Sources: The Hacker News · BleepingComputer · DuoCircle (week 42)*

### October Patch Tuesday: Microsoft fixes a record of more than 1,400 flaws

**Patches · Worth watching**

**What happened**
On 13 October, Microsoft fixed about 1,400 vulnerabilities (the total varies depending on how they are counted), compared with 973 in September. There are three zero-days, including an already exploited Windows shell flaw (CVE-2026-96951, score 8.8, a security-feature bypass).

**Why it matters**
Flaws that require no click in the mail client are highly sought after by attackers. Such a volume also weighs on the teams who have to test and then deploy the patches.

**Key takeaway**
Patch Tuesday takes place on the second Tuesday of each month. The next one falls on 10 November 2026.

*Sources: IT-Connect · Le Monde Informatique*

### NIS2: France still has not transposed the directive

**Regulation · Worth watching**

**What happened**
The "Résilience" bill, which transposes NIS2 as well as the REC and DORA directives, was once again removed from the National Assembly's agenda on 6 October. Update: the text returned to the Assembly on 9 November, with a formal vote on 17 November. The European deadline was 17 October 2024, and the European Commission has referred the matter to the Court of Justice of the EU.

**Why it matters**
NIS2 extends cybersecurity obligations to thousands of organisations (healthcare, water, local authorities, industry, etc.) with governance, risk management and incident reporting. For anyone aiming at a GRC (governance, risk and compliance) role, it is the number one regulatory project in France.

**Key takeaway**
Organisations can already check whether they will be an "essential entity" or an "important entity" and prepare their incident reporting processes.

*Sources: Leto · Next*

## Glossary

- **Zero-day**: a flaw exploited by attackers before the vendor has released a fix. Defenders therefore have "zero days" to prepare.
- **RCE**: Remote Code Execution: the attacker makes a machine they do not own run their own commands. It is the worst type of flaw.
- **Supply chain**: instead of attacking the target directly, an attacker compromises a supplier or a trusted tool to reach all its customers at once.
- **DDoS**: denial-of-service attack: a site is flooded with requests to make it unreachable. Disruptive, but no data is stolen.
