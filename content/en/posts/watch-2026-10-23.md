## The essentials

1. PHP2Shell: Amazon saw a first attack as early as 17 October, before the fixes were published. Millions of attempts followed, but no successful takeover is confirmed at this stage.
2. Trusted tools remain targeted: a Chinese group used AI agents to attack via ConnectWise, and the AI library LiteLLM was published booby-trapped for about two hours.
3. In France, the French Shooting Federation confirms its leak and Ircantec data is being put up for sale. In Africa, Interpol announces 1,462 arrests.

### PHP2Shell: attacks had begun before the fixes

**Vulnerability · Critical · Follow-up**

**What happened**
Amazon spotted a first exploitation attempt on 17 October in its honeypots (decoy systems), four days before the fixes were published. Cloudflare now counts millions of attempts. Relatively good news: no successful code execution is confirmed so far, but millions of PHP servers remain exposed (the exact figure varies with the measurement).

**Why it matters**
When a flaw leaks before its fix, the delay between the alert and the attack drops to zero. CERT-FR (the incident response centre of ANSSI, the French national cybersecurity agency) proposes temporary measures (disable session.upload_progress, filter certain "multipart" requests) while waiting for the update. For Blue Teams, Amazon's guidance is to hunt for traces of attack from 17 October, not only from the 21st.

**Key takeaway**
For a critical flaw exposed on the internet, patch delay is counted in hours, not days. And the search for compromise must go back to before the disclosure date.

*Sources: AWS Security Blog · The Hacker News · CERT-FR*

### A Chinese group attacks via ConnectWise with AI agents

**Espionage / State actors · Important**

**What happened**
Microsoft attributes to the group Storm-0940, linked to the Chinese state, a campaign launched in September. The attackers used stolen credentials from the ConnectWise Cloud Backup service (backup for Microsoft 365 and Google Workspace) to get into its customers' environments. They were helped by ARTEX, an open-source tool that chains AI agents together to run an attack almost without humans. In several cases, they obtained administrator rights in under three hours.

**Why it matters**
The victims are in government, defence, finance and energy. This is a supply-chain attack: a trusted service provider is used as the way in. AI speeds up the attack (about 2,900 login attempts per minute), which leaves human defenders little time.

**Key takeaway**
Third-party applications connected to Microsoft 365 often have very broad permissions. They must be inventoried, their permissions limited and their sign-ins monitored. Against automated attacks, detection and response must be automated too.

*Sources: The Record*

### LiteLLM: the AI library published with a secret stealer

**Supply chain · Important**

**What happened**
On 21 October, the release pipeline of LiteLLM (a widely used Python library for calling AI models) was compromised. Version 1.107.0 on PyPI and several Docker images contained malware for about two hours. It steals cloud credentials, AI API keys, SSH keys and browser passwords, then installs a program that downloads further payloads.

**Why it matters**
This is the second compromise of LiteLLM in 2026, after the one in March attributed to the TeamPCP group. It adds to OVHcloud, AOMEI and Railway: developer tools have become a priority target. The malware runs as soon as Python starts, even just to inspect the installation.

**Key takeaway**
If you installed 1.107.0: isolate the machine, roll back to 1.106.2 and change ALL secrets. Removing the package is not enough. For companies, pinning dependency versions prevents automatically installing a booby-trapped release.

*Sources: BleepingComputer*

### Leaks in France: the Shooting Federation confirms, Ircantec targeted in turn

**Data breach · Important · Follow-up**

**What happened**
Reminder: on 17 October, several leaks were claimed in France. On 22 October, the Fédération française de tir (French Shooting Federation) confirmed the attack, filed a complaint and informed ANSSI and the CNIL (the French data protection authority). About 64,000 ID photos and 19,000 documents belonging to licence holders are affected, but no banking data.

**Why it matters**
Confirmation by the victim changes the status of a leak: it moves from rumour to fact. An affected organisation must notify the CNIL within 72 hours and inform the individuals if the risk is high. This is the core of GRC work during an incident.

**Key takeaway**
Licence holders and members: be wary of emails or calls that quote your real information, that is the next risk. A hacker's claim remains a claim until the organisation has confirmed it.

*Sources: Police & Réalités · Fuites Infos*

### GitLab fixes a flaw rated 10/10

**Patches · Important**

**What happened**
On 21 October, GitLab released a fix for CVE-2026-110264, rated 10 out of 10. It can allow code to be executed on the server without being logged in, via a booby-trapped regular expression. It affects self-managed installations going back to 2014. No exploitation has been reported so far.

**Why it matters**
GitLab hosts companies' source code and deployment secrets: compromising it opens the door to everything else. This is already the second 10/10 flaw of this type in October.

**Key takeaway**
Update to a fixed version (19.5.1, 19.4.3, 19.3.6…) without waiting for exploitation. Plan for a short outage: some updates modify the database.

*Sources: The Hacker News*

### Interpol: 1,462 arrests in Africa against online scams

**Justice and law enforcement · Worth watching**

**What happened**
Operation Serengeti III, run until 31 August in 20 African countries with the United States, was announced on 22 October. Results: 1,462 arrests, nearly 11,000 malicious networks dismantled and more than 105 million dollars recovered. The identified victims (nearly 66,000) had lost more than 385 million dollars.

**Why it matters**
The scams targeted also affect Europe: fake investment sites, CEO fraud (a fake-transfer email scam) and phishing. These operations show that international cooperation can reach networks that seem out of reach.

**Key takeaway**
An investment platform promising big, quick gains is almost always a scam. When in doubt, check the AMF (the French financial markets authority) blacklists before paying anything.

*Sources: The Record · Clubic*

## Glossary

- **Botnet**: a network of hacked devices (servers, cameras, routers) remotely controlled by an attacker, often to launch DDoS attacks. Mirai is the best known.
- **Honeypot**: a fake system deliberately exposed to attract attackers and observe their techniques at no real risk.
- **CI/CD pipeline**: an automated chain that tests, builds and publishes software with every code change. If it is compromised, every new release can be booby-trapped.
- **Claim**: a hacker's assertion that they stole data or attacked a target. It is not proof until the victim or an investigator has confirmed it.
