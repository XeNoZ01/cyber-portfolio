*Weekly bulletin, week of 2 to 8 November 2026.*

## The essentials

1. The campaign against connected fridges is spreading: six European countries and plasma freezers of the EFS (the French blood establishment) were targeted on the night of 1 to 2 November, and US agencies issued an alert.
2. On the vulnerability side, OpenSSH ("sshole"), WordPress and Zyxel switches are being exploited: updates are the priority.
3. In France, a giant leak is claimed at SGS (vehicle inspection), and the RN confirms its own.
4. Binance reveals that North Korean attackers got in as early as June via employee workstations, while Chainalysis admits it exposed the access credentials of all its customers.

### Sabotaged fridges: second wave in Europe, EFS hit, US alert

**Industrial attack · Critical · Follow-up**

**What happened**: reminder: in October, more than 400 French supermarkets had seen their fridges forced into defrost mode. On the night of 1 to 2 November, a new wave hit stores in Belgium (Delhaize, Carrefour, Intermarché, Spar), Ireland, the Netherlands, Sweden, Estonia and Luxembourg, again through Danfoss controllers. The same night, plasma freezers of the Établissement français du sang, managed by the provider Coolworld, received a setpoint of 110 °C; the Ministry of Health says the incident was detected in time. On 5 November, CISA, the FBI and three other US agencies published a joint advisory on hardening refrigeration systems.

**Why it matters**: this is no longer an isolated incident: it is a campaign targeting the cold chain, all the way to the health sector. The company Lupovis saw, through honeypots, 276 reconnaissance attempts between 20 and 30 October, and mentions a Russian lead without being able to prove it. No one has claimed responsibility so far; the Belgian federal prosecutor's office is investigating.

**Key takeaway**: an industrial (OT) device must never have its administration interface open to the Internet. CERT-FR (the French government's computer emergency response team) and CISA give the same reflexes: cut the exposure, go through a VPN, change default passwords, bound temperature setpoints and alert on any excursion.

*Sources: Euronews · IT-Connect · IT-Connect · Euronews · CERT-FR*

### OpenSSH "sshole": passwordless login on Alpine and some lightweight Linux systems

**Vulnerability · Critical**

**What happened**: an OpenSSH flaw (CVE-2026-123158, score 8.1) allows logging in to an account whose password is disabled, often root, by typing any very long password (257 to 1,024 characters). It affects Linux systems based on the musl library without PAM: Alpine (versions 3.16 to 3.24), OpenWrt, Buildroot, Yocto. OpenSSH 10.6p2 fixes the problem, released as an emergency on 5-6 November. According to Previdian, cited by The Hacker News, an attacker obtained root access on a honeypot less than 9 hours after publication.

**Why it matters**: Alpine is everywhere in Docker containers, and OpenWrt in routers and connected devices. Remote root access without a password is the worst-case scenario. For a Blue Team analyst, this is typically the kind of alert where affected machines must be inventoried quickly.

**Key takeaway**: update OpenSSH, then restart sshd. In the meantime, disable password login and allow only SSH keys. A "password disabled" configuration is no protection if the software that checks it is buggy.

*Sources: SecurityWeek · The Hacker News*

### Vehicle inspection: a claimed leak of 22.7 million people at SGS (Sécuritest)

**Data leak · Important**

**What happened**: on 8 November, a database presented as that of the French branch of SGS (Sécuritest, Auto Sécurité, Vérif'Autos) was put up for sale on a cybercriminal forum. The seller claims 22.7 million people, 90.8 million vehicles and 74.7 million inspections; these figures are unverified. The sample analyzed links names, addresses and phone numbers to license plates, and also contains inspection-center access credentials with plaintext passwords, and IBANs. SGS has not confirmed; cyberattaque.org attributes the publication to ChimeraZ.

**Why it matters**: if confirmed, this would be one of the biggest French leaks of the year. A file linking a person to their car and address is ideal for credible scams (fake inspection reminder, fake fine).

**Key takeaway**: a claim is not proof: we wait for the company's confirmation. But for individuals, be wary right now of SMS or emails quoting your plate or your last inspection. On the company side, plaintext stored passwords remain a serious fault.

*Sources: Fuites Infos · Cyberattaque.org*

### Chainalysis breached: all customers' identifiers and tokens exposed

**Attack · Important**

**What happened**: Chainalysis, the company that helps police and banks trace money in cryptocurrencies, reported an incident on 30 October. On 4 November, it acknowledged that the names, identifiers, password hashes and active tokens of all its users had been exposed. Customer accounts of its KYT tool were accessed by the attacker; several services were taken offline. Chainalysis says the incident is not linked to the Binance theft.

**Why it matters**: Chainalysis claims more than 1,500 customers in 70 countries, including banks and investigators. The first announcements downplayed the incident before being corrected: a textbook case of evolving crisis communication.

**Key takeaway**: after a token leak, changing the password is not enough: you must revoke the tokens and replace API keys. A first statement saying "no evidence of access" is never final.

*Sources: American Banker*

### Binance: the attackers got in as early as June, via a fake job offer

**Espionage / state actors · Important · Follow-up**

**What happened**: reminder: $6.3 to 6.4 billion stolen on 29 October, attributed by the FBI to the North Korean group TraderTraitor. Binance now explains that one workstation had been compromised in June (a fake Homebrew installer while trying to install an AI coding tool), then another in September. According to Security Alliance, this second workstation was trapped by a fake recruitment approach. On 20 October, the attackers used an internal AI tool to take control of two engineers' workstations and found a deployment token. Kim Yo Jong denied any North Korean involvement, and laundering resumed via THORChain.

**Why it matters**: the biggest theft in history started with a personally targeted employee, not a technical flaw. For GRC, it is a strong argument for securing administrators' workstations and managing privileged tokens.

**Key takeaway**: fake job offers and fake technical tests are North Korean attackers' favorite weapon. A token that gives access to production must be short-lived, monitored and never stored on a workstation.

*Sources: Cointelegraph · CoinDesk · CoinDesk*

### WordPress: a core flaw exploited during updates

**Vulnerability · Important**

**What happened**: on 3 November, CISA added the WordPress flaw CVE-2026-119977 (score 8.1) to its KEV catalog. During a full core update, temporary files are placed at predictable addresses: an attacker who arrives at the right moment can run code without being logged in. The fix dates from the end of October. Wordfence counted more than 18,500 malicious requests in the first 21 hours.

**Why it matters**: WordPress runs a huge share of websites, including small local authorities and SMEs in France. A flaw in the core, rather than in a plugin, potentially concerns everyone.

**Key takeaway**: check that sites are up to date (version 7.1.4 according to several sources). An update is itself a sensitive moment: do it quickly and monitor access to temporary files.

*Sources: The Hacker News · CISA*

### Rassemblement national: the party confirms the cyberattack

**Data leak · To watch · Follow-up**

**What happened**: reminder: a file presented as the RN's member list had been published at the end of October. In a statement dated 4 November, the RN confirms that a membership-management tool was attacked and that part of some members' data leaked: names, contact details, occupations, year and amount of membership fee, local federation. The party gives no figure, has notified the CNIL (the French data protection authority) and filed a complaint with the Paris prosecutor's office.

**Why it matters**: after LFI, Renaissance, the PCF, the PRG and the Écologistes, another party has been hit within a few weeks. Here, unlike others, the leak is officially acknowledged.

**Key takeaway**: notifying the CNIL (within 72 hours) and informing the individuals concerned are GDPR obligations: this is exactly what this statement does. Members should expect targeted phishing.

*Sources: Rassemblement national (statement) · Génération NT*

### Zyxel: nearly 1,000 switches hacked through an unknown flaw

**Vulnerability · To watch**

**What happened**: on 3 November, Zyxel published an advisory on the flaw CVE-2026-107441 (score 8.8) in its GS1900 switches, fixed on 23 October. It allows commands to be executed without authentication. According to Binarly, it was used to hack nearly 1,000 devices, in attacks that were believed to be tied to another, already patched flaw.

**Why it matters**: it shows that an investigation can pick the wrong flaw: the devices were thought to be protected by the June fix, and they were not. Network equipment makes for discreet, little-monitored targets.

**Key takeaway**: update GS1900 switches and never expose their web interface to the Internet. After a compromise, check which flaw was really used before declaring the matter closed.

*Sources: SecurityWeek*

### NIS2: the Résilience bill returns to the National Assembly this week

**Regulation · To watch · Follow-up**

**What happened**: reminder: the text transposing NIS2 into French law had been removed from the agenda in early October. It is now scheduled for public session from Monday 9 to Friday 13 November (except the 11th). Deputies can still amend it, ahead of a possible joint committee (commission mixte paritaire) with the Senate.

**Why it matters**: France is more than two years behind the European deadline (17/10/2024). NIS2 will raise the regulated sectors from 6 to 18 and concern thousands of entities: it is the core of GRC work for the coming years.

**Key takeaway**: follow this week's vote. For a future GRC profile, knowing NIS2 (obligations, penalties, the role of ANSSI) is a real asset in interviews.

*Sources: Leto · Silicon.fr*

### Europol dismantles a network of fake investment sites

**Justice and law enforcement · To watch**

**What happened**: on 3 November, Europol announced an operation in 17 countries against a network of fake investment platforms, run from Cyprus and Israel with call centers in Albania, Kosovo and North Macedonia. More than 100 servers were seized and seven people arrested. Known losses exceed $4.5 million, with potential victims in more than 50 countries.

**Why it matters**: fake-investment scams remain among the costliest for the general public. The investigation started from a complaint by a German couple in 2022: filing a complaint really does help.

**Key takeaway**: an advisor who calls to offer a very profitable, quick investment is almost always a scammer. Check the AMF (the French financial markets authority) blacklists before any investment.

*Sources: CyberScoop*

## Glossary

- **OT (operational technology)**: systems that run the physical world: fridge controllers, factory automation, sensors. They are often old and poorly protected, while an attack has concrete effects.
- **Default credentials**: username and password shipped from the factory (e.g. "admin / 12345"). They are public, so they must be changed at installation.
- **Race condition**: a flaw that appears for a brief moment, when two operations overlap; the attacker has to act at the right time, for example during an update.
- **Reconnaissance**: the first phase of an attack: the attacker spots exposed machines and probes their weaknesses before striking.
- **Cryptocurrency laundering**: moving stolen money across thousands of addresses and currencies (bridges, exchanges) to blur the trail before cashing out.
