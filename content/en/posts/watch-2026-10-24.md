## The essentials

1. In France, the Communist Party (PCF) confirms the leak of its members' and supporters' file, and the French Sailing Federation announces more than 2 million records exposed.
2. On the network equipment side, Fortinet is pushing urgent patches without detailing the flaws, and an Arista VeloCloud zero-day is being exploited; one hacker has already stolen more than 70,000 credentials from VPNs and firewalls.
3. In Europe, the Russian group APT28 spied on a shipyard and a NATO military engine maker.

### Leaks in France: the PCF, the Sailing Federation and ENGIE affected

**Data breach · Important**

**What happened**
On 23 October, the Parti communiste français announced a leak of its members' and supporters' file, following an alert from ANSSI (the French national cybersecurity agency). The analysed file reportedly contains about 189,000 people (independent estimate, not confirmed by the party): names, addresses, phone numbers, emails, with no banking data. The same day, the Fédération française de voile (FFVoile, the French Sailing Federation) disclosed more than 2 million records exposed through a flaw in an internal tool, spotted on 19 October. ENGIE is also warning some customers of access to their data through a tool used by its sales partners.

**Why it matters**
Being on a party's file reveals a political opinion: this is "sensitive" data under the GDPR, more strictly protected. The three organisations notified the CNIL (the French data protection authority) or their customers, as the law requires. In GRC, these cases show the importance of the record of processing activities and the 72-hour notification procedure.

**Key takeaway**
With this data, scammers can write very convincing messages (your name, your club, your energy contract). Be wary of any email or text message that cites these organisations and demands a payment or a password. Leaks often go through a side tool or a service provider, not through the main system.

*Sources: PCF (press release) · Cyberattaque.org · Fuites Infos (FFVoile) · Fuites Infos (ENGIE)*

### Fortinet asks you to patch right now, without saying what it fixes

**Patches · Critical**

**What happened**
On 21 and 22 October, Fortinet released updates for FortiOS, FortiManager, FortiAnalyzer, FortiMail and FortiAuthenticator. The flaws are described as critical or high, but no CVE number or details will be published before 10 November. They were found with the help of cutting-edge AI models.

**Why it matters**
Fortinet explains that AI now makes it possible to compare the old and new versions of a piece of software to build an attack within hours. The delay between a patch and the first attacks is therefore shrinking sharply. FortiGate firewalls are very widespread and are a prime ransomware target.

**Key takeaway**
Update internet-facing equipment first (e.g. FortiOS 7.6.8 or 7.4.13). In the meantime, restrict access to the administration interface (HTTPS, SSH) to trusted IP addresses. A patch without details is still an urgent patch.

*Sources: IT-Connect*

### Arista VeloCloud: a zero-day flaw already exploited

**Vulnerability · Important**

**What happened**
On 22 October, Arista fixed a critical flaw (CVE-2026-114625) in VeloCloud Orchestrator, the console that manages wide-area enterprise networks (SD-WAN). It is an SQL injection: a user who is already logged in can become an administrator. Attacks are under way and CISA has added it to its KEV catalogue of exploited flaws.

**Why it matters**
A console that manages the whole network is an ideal target: taking it means controlling many sites at once. For a Blue Team analyst, Arista provides two attacker IP addresses to block and traces to look for in web logs.

**Key takeaway**
Move to 5.2.3.18, 6.1.3.10 or 6.4.2.12 (or later). Restrict access to the interface to the administration network and review recent administrator actions. If in doubt, keep the logs before repairing.

*Sources: BleepingComputer · Privacy Needle*

### The Russian group APT28 spies on a NATO shipyard

**Espionage / State actors · Important**

**What happened**
On 20 October, Symantec disclosed a campaign by APT28, a group linked to Russian military intelligence (GRU). A shipyard, a manufacturer of military aircraft engines and a police service in two European NATO countries were targeted. It all began on 24 July with a booby-trapped Word document opened by an employee, followed by the installation of the Hookedge backdoor, active until the end of September.

**Why it matters**
This is classic espionage against European defence, in the context of the war in Ukraine. The way in remains mundane: an attachment. The attackers stayed several weeks without being seen.

**Key takeaway**
A simple attachment can open the door to a state actor. On the defence side: raise phishing awareness, block macros, and look for the indicators of compromise (IOCs) published by Symantec. Undetected dwell time is what causes the most damage.

*Sources: The Record · Cybersecurity Dive*

### VPNs and firewalls: a hacker stole more than 70,000 credentials

**Ransomware · Important · Follow-up**

**What happened**
Reminder: the Palo Alto flaw CVE-2026-0257 is already being used by ransomware groups. New: Group-IB and NyxLab describe the hacker "k0t", who used it together with a FortiWeb flaw (CVE-2025-64446) and guessed passwords. More than 70,000 credentials were exposed; many victims then appear on the Qilin and The Gentlemen sites. Europe accounts for about 16% of victims.

**Why it matters**
k0t appears to be an initial access broker: it gets in, then resells access or hands it over to ransomware groups. This confirms that edge devices (VPNs, firewalls) are the first way in.

**Key takeaway**
Patch VPNs and firewalls, enforce MFA, and watch for unexpected remote access tools (MeshCentral, Atera, AnyDesk) and transfers to MEGA or Wasabi via Rclone.

*Sources: The Hacker News*

### PHP and GitLab: a new PHP flaw, and Ruby fixes the cause of the GitLab flaws

**Vulnerability · Worth watching · Follow-up**

**What happened**
Reminder: PHP2Shell and the 10/10 GitLab flaw were fixed on 21 October. New on the PHP side: on 23 October, PHP reported another flaw (CVE-2026-115560), with no fix yet, which only affects rare configurations; the setting session.upload_progress.enabled=0 blocks it. New on the GitLab side: Ruby released versions 4.0.9, 3.4.12 and 3.3.13, which fix the bugs in its regex engine behind four critical GitLab flaws; Ruby 3.2 and older will not be fixed.

**Why it matters**
A flaw in a language affects every application that uses it, well beyond a single product. Ruby did not create a CVE of its own: scanners that rely on CVEs may miss it.

**Key takeaway**
Keep the PHP setting already recommended for PHP2Shell. Update Ruby on servers, not just GitLab. An inventory of the language versions in use (SBOM) helps to know where to act.

*Sources: IT-Connect · BleepingComputer · The Hacker News*

## Glossary

- **SQL injection**: an attack that slips commands into a field or a web address so that they are executed by the site's database.
- **Backdoor**: a hidden program that lets an attacker return to a machine at will, without going through normal authentication.
- **Initial access broker (IAB)**: a hacker who specialises in breaking into networks and then resells that access to other groups, often ransomware gangs.
- **Patch diffing**: comparing software before and after a patch to spot the fixed flaw and build an attack against those who have not yet updated.
